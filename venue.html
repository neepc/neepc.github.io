Travel

Getting to Providence

By train

Providence Station is served by Amtrak (Northeast Regional and Acela) and by the MBTA Commuter Rail from Boston's South Station and Back Bay. From the station, campus is about a 15 minute walk uphill, or a short taxi ride.

By air

Rhode Island T.F. Green International Airport (PVD) is about 20 minutes south of campus by car. Boston Logan (BOS) is about an hour away; take the Silver Line to South Station, then the train to Providence.

By car

Providence is on I-95, about an hour from Boston and two and a half hours from New York. Parking near campus is limited. Parking details TBA.

Getting around

Campus, downtown and most of the places below are walkable. RIPTA buses run across the city, and rideshare is easy to find.

Things to Do in Providence

Stay for the evening

Providence is a small, walkable city with a big food scene. A few favorites near campus and beyond:

Thayer Street

The main street next to campus, with cafes, cheap eats and bookstores. The easy choice for lunch or a coffee between sessions.

Benefit Street

The "Mile of History": a walk past restored colonial houses on College Hill, along with the Providence Athenaeum, one of the oldest libraries in the country.

RISD Museum

The Rhode Island School of Design's art museum, a few minutes' walk down the hill, with collections from ancient art to contemporary design.

Prospect Terrace Park

A small park on College Hill with one of the best views over downtown and the State House. Good at sunset.

Federal Hill

Providence's Little Italy. Atwells Avenue is lined with Italian restaurants, bakeries and bars, and is the classic spot for a group dinner.

Fox Point and Wickenden Street

Coffee shops, small restaurants and vintage stores, a short walk from campus, ending at India Point Park on the water.

WaterFire

Bonfires lit on the rivers downtown, with music, on select evenings from late spring through fall. Check the WaterFire schedule to see if one falls on conference weekend.

Local tastes

Try coffee milk (the official state drink), a frozen Del's Lemonade, and a hot wiener "all the way" at a New York System diner.
